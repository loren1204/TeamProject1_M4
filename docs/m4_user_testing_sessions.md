# M4 User Testing Session Summaries

## Testing overview

The supplied interview summaries document completed human testing with **six
participants**: five undergraduate students and one graduate student,
George, representing the instructor perspective. George's evaluation is
reported separately and is excluded from the five-student M3 measures.

The notes list November 18, 2026 as the documentation date and Kaizen as the
testing environment. They do not record individual session dates or formats.
Feedback below is paraphrased from the notes; no verbatim quotes or numerical
ratings were supplied. Individual outcomes for the M3 criteria are included
where the notes provide them; otherwise, the result is explicitly marked
“Not recorded.”

## Undergraduate sessions

### Tester 1

- **Role/context:** Third-year Software Engineering student who regularly
  uses AI to study and understand unfamiliar concepts.
- **Date and format:** Not recorded.
- **Task given:** Use Kaizen to understand velocity and acceleration and
  receive guidance on a related Physics 101 problem.
- **What they did:**
  1. Entered the Student workspace and selected Physics 101.
  2. Explored the course-specific tutoring interface.
  3. Asked for an explanation of velocity and acceleration.
  4. Received a guiding question instead of the direct explanation expected.
  5. Rephrased the request for a simpler explanation.
  6. Continued with a related physics problem and reviewed the suggested
     reasoning step.
- **Where they got stuck:** The Tutor did not distinguish a general concept
  question clearly from a graded homework request.
- **Relevant participant feedback:** Course-based conversation organization
  was useful compared with general-purpose AI. Direct explanations would be
  preferable for general questions.
- **What surprised us:** The organization was useful even to a student who
  already uses AI; guiding questions could make a simple concept explanation
  unnecessarily complicated.
- **Would they use Kaizen again? What would make them more likely to?** A
  direct yes/no response was not recorded. More direct explanations could
  make it more useful for studying and exam preparation.
- **Ratings:** Not recorded.
- **M3 outcomes:** Independent task completion, next-step explanation,
  willingness to reuse, critical-policy outcome, and understanding of
  tutoring restrictions were not recorded or formally assessed.
- **Related evaluation case:** m3_001 is related to direct concept
  explanations, but its test asks about speed and velocity, not velocity and
  acceleration.

### Tester 3

- **Role/context:** Third-year Biology student with limited AI experience
  because of concerns about academic integrity and instructor policies.
- **Date and format:** Not recorded.
- **Task given:** Get a biological concept explanation and identify the next
  reasoning step in a genetics problem.
- **What they did:**
  1. Reviewed the Python tutoring agent.
  2. Requested an explanation of cell membrane structure and function.
  3. Explored the agent's approach to a genetics problem.
  4. Reviewed the Planner-to-Tutor workflow.
  5. Moved to the Kaizen Student workspace.
  6. Explored Workspace Settings and available privacy information.
  7. Considered instructor access to tutoring conversations.
- **Where they got stuck:** Planner/Tutor terminology was not immediately
  meaningful, and the interface did not clearly establish what information
  was stored or whether instructors could see conversations.
- **Relevant participant feedback:** Instructor approval and transparency
  about data use matter to students concerned about academic-integrity
  expectations.
- **What surprised us:** Instructor approval and privacy transparency may be
  as important to adoption as response quality; policy restrictions can
  encourage cautious students to use course-specific AI.
- **Would they use Kaizen again? What would make them more likely to?** A
  direct yes/no response was not recorded. Explicit instructor approval and
  clear privacy information would make it more approachable.
- **Ratings:** Not recorded.
- **M3 outcomes:** Independent completion, next-step explanation, reuse,
  critical-policy outcome, and understanding of restrictions were not
  recorded or formally assessed.
- **Related evaluation cases:** m3_006 and m3_007.

### Tester 5

- **Role/context:** Fourth-year Computer Science student experienced with
  multiple AI platforms.
- **Date and format:** Not recorded.
- **Task given:** Upload a free-body diagram and determine whether Kaizen can
  provide guidance specific to the uploaded work.
- **What they did:**
  1. Entered the Student workspace and selected Physics 101.
  2. Uploaded a diagram showing gravity, normal force, applied force, and
     friction.
  3. Requested feedback on the represented forces.
  4. Checked whether the response referenced visual information.
  5. Explored a friction follow-up and compared image-based with text-based
     tutoring.
- **Where they got stuck:** The interface did not make clear whether uploaded
  content was received and analyzed. The documented implementation does not
  send image contents to the tutoring model.
- **Relevant participant feedback:** The evaluation emphasized the need for
  image-specific feedback rather than a general explanation.
- **What surprised us:** An attachment appearing in the interface does not
  establish that the model understood it; this can reduce confidence in other
  features.
- **Would they use Kaizen again? What would make them more likely to?** A
  direct yes/no response was not recorded. Reliable image analysis and
  course-specific guidance were identified as adoption factors.
- **Ratings:** Not recorded.
- **M3 outcomes:** Independent completion was not formally assessed; the
  image limitation was identified. Next-step explanation, reuse,
  critical-policy outcome, and understanding of restrictions were not
  recorded or formally assessed.
