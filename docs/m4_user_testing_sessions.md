# M4 User Testing Session Summaries

## Testing overview

The M4 interviews took place **November 13, 2026, in person**, and included
**six participants**: five undergraduate students and one graduate student,
George, representing the instructor perspective. George's evaluation is
reported separately and is excluded from the five-student M3 measures. The
documentation date is November 18, 2026; the testing environment was the
Kaizen web application.

Feedback below is paraphrased from the interview summaries; no verbatim
quotes or numerical participant ratings were supplied.

## Participant overview

| Tester | Role/context | Evaluation focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Junior, Software Engineering | Concept explanations and tutoring |
| 2 | Sophomore, Pre-Med | Homework assistance and academic integrity |
| 3 | Junior, Biology | Privacy, transparency, and academic integrity |
| 4 | Sophomore, Engineering | Accessibility, practice, and conversation persistence |
| 5 | Senior, Computer Science | Image analysis and AI reliability |
| 6 | Master's student, Data Science and AI; Dendritic Institute | Instructor functionality and educational analytics |

## Undergraduate sessions

### Tester 1

- **Role/context:** Third-year Software Engineering student who regularly
  uses AI to study and understand unfamiliar concepts.
- **Date and format:** November 13, 2026, in person.
- **Task given:** Use Kaizen to understand velocity and acceleration and
  receive guidance on a related Physics 101 problem.
- **What they did:**
  1. Entered the Student workspace and selected Physics 101.
  2. Explored the course-specific tutoring interface.
  3. Asked for an explanation of velocity and acceleration.
  4. Received a guiding question instead of the direct explanation expected.
  5. Rephrased the request for a simpler explanation.
  6. Continued with a related physics problem.
  7. Explained the suggested reasoning step without facilitator assistance.
- **Where they got stuck:** The Tutor did not distinguish a general concept
  request clearly from graded homework assistance.
- **Relevant participant feedback:** Course-based conversation organization
  was useful compared with general-purpose AI. Direct explanations were
  preferred for general questions.
- **What surprised us:** Course organization was useful even to a frequent AI
  user, while guiding questions could make a simple concept explanation
  unnecessarily complicated.
- **Would they use Kaizen again? What would make them more likely to?** Yes.
  Direct concept explanations would improve its usefulness for studying and
  exam preparation.
- **Ratings:** Numerical satisfaction rating not recorded.
- **M3 outcomes:** Completed independently: Yes. Explained next step: Yes.
  Would reuse: Yes. Received a restricted full answer: No. Understood tutoring
  restrictions: Yes.
- **Related evaluation case:** m3_001 is related to direct concept
  explanations, but its test asks about speed and velocity, not velocity and
  acceleration.

### Tester 2

- **Role/context:** Second-year Pre-Med student taking Chemistry 201 who
  occasionally uses AI for independent study or difficult assignments.
- **Date and format:** November 13, 2026, in person.
- **Task given:** Get help checking a stoichiometry assignment without having
  the AI complete the graded problem.
- **What they did:**
  1. Entered the Chemistry 201 Student workspace.
  2. Navigated to Assignments and reviewed the image-attachment feature.
  3. Requested help checking a stoichiometry problem.
  4. Asked for a direct solution to evaluate the tutoring restriction.
  5. Received guidance instead of a complete answer.
  6. Requested a similar example and reviewed how it could apply to the
     assignment.
  7. Explained the next reasoning step successfully.
- **Where they got stuck:** The reason for withholding the final answer was
  unclear, and it was uncertain whether handwritten work was analyzed.
- **Relevant participant feedback:** Similar examples were useful as an
  alternative to solving the assigned problem. The Assignments organization
  felt familiar from Canvas, and assistance outside class was beneficial.
- **What surprised us:** A related example can continue to help while
  respecting academic-integrity restrictions; familiar course organization
  may reduce confusion.
- **Would they use Kaizen again? What would make them more likely to?** Yes.
  Chemistry assignments and independent exam preparation were identified as
  useful contexts, especially when an instructor is unavailable.
- **Ratings:** Numerical satisfaction rating not recorded.
- **M3 outcomes:** Completed independently: Yes. Explained next step: Yes.
  Would reuse: Yes. Received a restricted full answer: No. Understood tutoring
  restrictions: Yes.
- **Related evaluation cases:** m3_004 and m3_005. Human review passed both;
  m3_004 remains an automated keyword failure.

### Tester 3

- **Role/context:** Third-year Biology student with limited AI experience
  because of academic-integrity and instructor-policy concerns.
- **Date and format:** November 13, 2026, in person.
- **Task given:** Get a biological concept explanation and identify the next
  reasoning step in a genetics problem.
- **What they did:**
  1. Reviewed the Python tutoring agent.
  2. Requested an explanation of cell membrane structure and function.
  3. Explored the agent's approach to a genetics problem.
  4. Reviewed the Planner-to-Tutor workflow.
  5. Required facilitator assistance during the session.
  6. Transitioned to the Kaizen Student workspace.
  7. Explored Workspace Settings and available privacy information.
  8. Considered instructor access to tutoring conversations.
  9. Explained the next reasoning step successfully.
- **Where they got stuck:** Planner/Tutor terminology was not immediately
  meaningful, and the interface did not clearly establish what information
  was stored or whether instructors could see conversations. Jordan required
  facilitator assistance and did not meet the independent-completion
  criterion.
- **Relevant participant feedback:** Instructor approval and transparency
  about data use matter to students concerned about academic-integrity
  expectations.
- **What surprised us:** Instructor approval and privacy transparency may be
  as important to adoption as response quality. Restrictions may encourage
  cautious students to use course-specific AI.
- **Would they use Kaizen again? What would make them more likely to?** Yes.
  Explicit instructor approval and clear privacy information would make it
  more approachable.
- **Ratings:** Numerical satisfaction rating not recorded.
- **M3 outcomes:** Completed independently: No. Explained next step: Yes.
  Would reuse: Yes. Received a restricted full answer: No. Understood tutoring
  restrictions: Yes.
- **Related evaluation cases:** m3_006 and m3_007.

### Tester 4

- **Role/context:** Second-year Engineering student who uses personal and
  university computers and prefers customizable interface settings.
- **Date and format:** November 13, 2026, in person.
- **Task given:** Explore the Student workspace, review conversation-resume
  functionality, and complete a physics practice activity.
- **What they did:**
  1. Entered Kaizen and reviewed the introductory animation.
  2. Explored accessibility preferences in Workspace Settings.
  3. Selected Physics 101 and reviewed how previous conversations could be
     resumed.

  5. Reviewed the explanation and suggested next step.

  7. Completed the assigned tutoring task independently and explained the

- **Where they got stuck:** Accessibility preferences were not immediately

  the experience.
- **What surprised us:** Practice can support exam preparation beyond
  conversation. Accessibility and persistence may affect continued use even
  when tutoring responses are useful.
- **Would they use Kaizen again? What would make them more likely to?** Yes.
  Practice activities and account-based conversation access would improve
  the experience.
- **Ratings:** Numerical satisfaction rating not recorded.
- **M3 outcomes:** Completed independently: Yes. Explained next step: Yes.
  Would reuse: Yes. Received a restricted full answer: No. Understood tutoring

### Tester 5

- **Role/context:** Fourth-year Computer Science student experienced with
  multiple AI platforms.
- **Date and format:** November 13, 2026, in person.
- **Task given:** Upload a free-body diagram and determine whether Kaizen can
  provide guidance specific to the uploaded work.
- **What they did:**
  1. Entered the Student workspace and selected Physics 101.
  2. Uploaded a diagram showing gravity, normal force, applied force, and
     friction.
  3. Requested feedback on the represented forces.
  4. Checked whether the response referenced visual information.
  5. Explored a friction follow-up and compared image-based with text-based
     tutoring.
  6. Could not complete the image-specific task or explain its intended next
     step independently.
- **Where they got stuck:** The interface did not make clear whether uploaded
  content was received and analyzed. The documented implementation does not
  send image contents to the tutoring model.
- **Relevant participant feedback:** Image-specific feedback was important;
  a general explanation could be given without the attachment.
- **What surprised us:** An attachment appearing in the interface does not
  establish that the model understood it; this can reduce confidence in other
  features.
- **Would they use Kaizen again? What would make them more likely to?** No,
  not in its current state. Reliable image analysis and course-specific
  guidance would improve the likelihood of reuse.
- **Ratings:** Numerical satisfaction rating not recorded.
- **M3 outcomes:** Completed independently: No. Explained next step: No.
  Would reuse: No. Received a restricted full answer: No. Understood tutoring
  restrictions: Yes.

## Additional instructor-perspective evaluation

### Tester 6

- **Role/context:** Master's student in Data Science and Artificial
  Intelligence involved with the Dendritic Institute. Experience includes
  digital education, Canvas course development, and educational analytics.
- **Date and format:** November 13, 2026, in person.
- **Task given:** Evaluate how Kaizen could support instructors and supplement
  existing Canvas courses.
- **What they did:**
  1. Reviewed the Instructor workspace and course organization.
  2. Compared course management with Canvas.
  3. Reviewed assignment-level restrictions and how hints or examples could
     be permitted while complete solutions are restricted.
  4. Considered policy application across student accounts.
  5. Reviewed the analytics dashboard and useful student-performance
     information.
  6. Considered how Kaizen could supplement a course without requiring
     instructors to recreate content.
- **Where they got stuck:** Policies need consistent cross-account
  application; analytics should reflect meaningful student activity; and
  instructors should not need to duplicate course content.
- **Relevant participant feedback:** The evaluation distinguished student
  engagement from learning and identified recurring difficulties, practice
  outcomes, and improvement over time as potentially useful measures.
- **What surprised us:** The instructor perspective shifted attention from
  conversation volume toward educational outcomes and course-content
  improvement.
- **Would they recommend Kaizen? What would make them more likely to?** A
  direct yes/no recommendation was not recorded. Policy enforcement,
  meaningful analytics, and compatibility with existing workflows were
  identified as adoption factors.
- **Ratings:** Numerical rating not recorded.
- **M3 outcomes:** Not applicable; George is excluded from the undergraduate
  student criteria.

## M3 undergraduate success criteria

The updated notes confirm the participant-level outcomes for all five
undergraduate students. George is excluded.

| Criterion | Target | Actual result | Status |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Complete the task without facilitator assistance | At least 4/5 | 3/5 (60%) | Not met |
| Explain the next reasoning step | At least 4/5 | 4/5 (80%) | Met |
| Would use the agent again | At least 3/5 | 4/5 (80%) | Met |
| Critical policy violations | Zero | 0/5 received a restricted full answer | Met |
| Understand the agent guides rather than completes work | At least 4/5 | 5/5 (100%) | Met |

**Overall: 4/5 student criteria (80%) were met.** Independent task
completion was the only criterion not met: Tester 3 required facilitator
assistance and Tester 5 could not complete the image-specific task. All five
understood the tutoring restrictions, and none received a restricted full
answer.

These usability measures are separate from the automated evaluation and the
9/10 human-review score of agent responses.
