SCOPE DOWN is the recommended decision. CourseGuide AI addresses a continuing
need for accessible STEM help that follows instructor policies, and M4 shows
improvement, but reliability and core usability still need work.

The M3 automated baseline was 4/10 (40%); M4 reached 6/10 (60%), a
20-percentage-point improvement. The original automated target was 8/10
(80%), so it was not met. Human review separately passed 9/10 (90%). The
CSV confirms that human review failed m3_001 and passed m3_003, m3_004, and
m3_007 despite their automated keyword failures. In m3_004, the Tutor avoided
giving the answer and offered a hint, but Tester 2's interview notes identify
unclear refusal wording. This is a communication concern, not evidence of a
critical policy violation.

Updated notes document completed evaluations with five undergraduate
students and one additional graduate student, George, representing the
instructor perspective. Testers 1–3 raised needs around direct explanations,
clear restrictions, privacy, and understandable terminology; Testers 4–5
focused on accessibility, conversation continuity, and image analysis.
George emphasized consistent policies, meaningful learning analytics, and
fit with existing course workflows. The notes do not include session dates
or formats, ratings, or participant-level counts for the M3 criteria, so
those success measures cannot yet be scored. George is not part of the
five-student denominator.

Pivot was rejected because the educational problem remains relevant and the
evaluation improved; there is not enough evidence that a different product
direction would work better. Proceeding with the full scope was rejected
because the automated threshold was missed, m3_001 remains a behavioral
failure, and image analysis, persistence, privacy communication, and
instructor functionality need improvement or further validation. The
interviews support these priorities but do not establish that the M3
usability targets were met.

The narrowed scope should retain Physics 101, Chemistry 201, and Biology 201;
course-specific tutoring; reliable academic-integrity enforcement; direct
concept explanations; similar examples; bounded homework hints; and the
essential student workspace. Validate image handling, privacy messaging, and
conversation persistence. George's evaluation supports considering Kaizen as
a supplement to Canvas, not a replacement. Keep advanced analytics and broad
LMS integrations as later opportunities, and record the missing dates,
formats, ratings, and criterion-level outcomes before claiming measured
usability success.
