SCOPE DOWN is the recommended decision. CourseGuide AI addresses a continuing
need for accessible STEM help that follows instructor policies, and M4 shows
improvement, but reliability and core usability still need work.

The M3 automated baseline was 4/10 (40%); M4 reached 6/10 (60%), a
20-percentage-point improvement. The original automated target was 8/10
(80%), so it was not met. Human review separately passed 9/10 (90%). The
CSV confirms human review failed m3_001 and passed m3_003, m3_004, and m3_007
despite their automated keyword failures. Keyword scoring missed three
acceptable responses, while human review retained m3_001 as a behavioral
failure. In m3_004, the Tutor withheld the answer and offered a useful hint,
though Tester 2's feedback identifies unclear refusal wording. This is a
communication concern, not evidence of a critical policy violation.

In-person evaluations on November 13, 2026 involved five undergraduates and
George, whose instructor perspective is supplementary. Four of five
undergraduate criteria were met: next-step explanation and reuse each scored
4/5 (80%), all five understood restrictions, and no student received a
restricted answer. Independent task completion was 3/5 (60%), below its 4/5
target. Testers raised needs around direct explanations, privacy,
accessibility, conversation continuity, and image analysis. George emphasized
consistent policies, meaningful analytics, and course-workflow fit. Ratings
were not recorded; George is excluded from student counts.

Pivot was rejected because the educational problem remains relevant and the
evaluation improved; there is not enough evidence that a different product
direction would work better. Proceeding with the full scope was rejected
because the automated threshold was missed, m3_001 remains a behavioral
failure, and independent completion also fell short. Image analysis,
persistence, privacy communication, and instructor functionality need
improvement or further validation. The 90% human-review rate is numerically
above the 80% target, but it does not replace the unmet automated threshold
or negate the student-testing completion result.

The narrowed scope should retain Physics 101, Chemistry 201, and Biology 201;
course-specific tutoring; academic-integrity enforcement; direct concept
explanations; similar examples; bounded homework hints; and the essential
student workspace. Validate image handling, privacy messaging, and
persistence. Treat Kaizen as a Canvas supplement, not a replacement; keep
advanced analytics and broad LMS integrations for later. The 90% human-review
result is not a student satisfaction score.
