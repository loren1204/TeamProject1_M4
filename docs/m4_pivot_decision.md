SCOPE DOWN is the recommended decision. CourseGuide AI addresses a continuing
need for accessible STEM help that follows instructor policies, and the M4
results show improvement, but core reliability and usability still need work.

The M3 automated baseline was 4/10 (40%); M4 reached 6/10 (60%), a
20-percentage-point improvement. The original automated target was 8/10
(80%), so it was not met. Task 1's separate human review passed 9/10 (90%).
That review overturned the keyword-only misses in m3_003, m3_004, and m3_007
while confirming m3_001's missing concept explanation. In m3_004, the Tutor
did not use the expected answer/solution keywords or state the exact refusal,
but it avoided giving the answer and offered a hint. This is a communication
gap, not evidence of a critical policy violation.

The user-testing evidence does not establish a usability result. No completed
session notes were available for the five undergraduate testers, and the
actual number who completed testing remains [TO FILL]. Tester 1 through
Tester 5 outcomes are unrecorded; Tester 6 (George) is a proposed
instructor-perspective evaluation, not a completed session. The supplementary
walkthroughs raise hypotheses about direct concept explanations, clear
academic-integrity limits, image attachments, conversation persistence,
privacy, instructor policy persistence, and analytics. These are not actual
participant findings. George's proposed review raises whether Kaizen should
supplement Canvas rather than try to replace an established learning
management system.

Pivot was rejected because the educational problem remains relevant, the
automated results show measurable improvement, and there is not enough
evidence that a different product direction would work better. Proceeding
with the full scope was rejected because the automated target was missed,
refusal wording remains inconsistent, several web capabilities are
prototypes, and student and instructor needs have not been validated. Adding
more features now could make the product harder to test without improving
its core reliability.

The narrowed scope should retain Physics 101, Chemistry 201, and Biology 201;
course-specific tutoring; reliable academic-integrity enforcement; direct
concept explanations; similar examples; bounded homework hints; and a
functional student workspace. Validate image handling, privacy messaging,
and conversation persistence before relying on them. Keep advanced analytics
and broad Canvas integrations as future opportunities, and complete the
undergraduate sessions and George's separate evaluation before making
user-validation claims.
